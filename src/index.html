01Research

Reading the magnetic field

Five threads, one question asked at five heights: what is the field doing in the solar atmosphere, and how would we know?

01

Spectropolarimetric inversion

A Stokes profile is a fingerprint with the field, the temperature and the velocity tangled together. I write inversion codes that untangle them: Milne–Eddington solved in batch on a GPU, node-based nonlinear least squares, and now physics-informed networks that return a whole atmosphere in a single pass.

A&A 710, A227 · CuSP · SPOT
02

Learning the inverse problem

The inverse problem is expensive because it is iterative. A network trained on radiative-transfer solutions does not replace the physics; it makes the physics affordable, returning a full field of view without iterating per pixel.

ApJ 1001, 96 · A&A 710, A227
03

Magnetic-field extrapolation

The photosphere supplies a boundary condition and the corona supplies the consequences. Extrapolation lifts a measured surface field into the volume above it, force-free in the classical formulation and now learned with a physics-reinforced GAN.

ApJ 1001, 96 · PF2nlfff
04

Data-constrained MHD

A simulation is only useful if it can be observed. I run MHD models of the global corona constrained by measured fields, and synthesise what an instrument would see, so that model and observation can be compared in the same units.

ApJ 997, 228 · ApJ 1004, 166
05

Coronal seismology

Loops oscillate, and the period carries density, field strength and geometry inside it. Spatial seismology turns that oscillation back into the parameters responsible for it: a measurement made of time instead of light.

A&A 664, A48 · A&A 678, A205

04About

Between the observation and the model

I work between the observation and the model. The instrument measures polarisation; the atmosphere answers with a magnetic field, a temperature and a line-of-sight velocity. Everything I do is an attempt to get from the first to the second without losing the physics on the way.

I am a direct-entry PhD student in astronomy at Nanjing University, where the coronal-seismology work began, and a guest researcher at the Rosseland Centre for Solar Physics in Oslo, where I work on inversion, extrapolation and radiation synthesis for data-constrained MHD models of the corona.

The codes are public, SPOT and CuSP for inversion and PF2nlfff for extrapolation, because a diagnostic is only as reproducible as the code that produces it and because the fastest way to be corrected is to publish the tool.
